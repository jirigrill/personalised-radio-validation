# 12 — MRR arithmetic: what does $1–3k actually require?

Type: research
Status: open
Blocked by: 09, 11

## Question

The arithmetic behind the bar. **Method carries over** from
`../podcasts-notes-validation/docs/research/07-mrr-arithmetic.md` — reuse its structure and sources
rather than reinventing them — but **re-run every number** at this concept's bar and category.

1. **The base rate.** RevenueCat's most recent State of Subscription Apps: what fraction of
   newly-launched apps reach **$1k/mo** and **$3k/mo**, in the relevant category (Music/Audio or
   Entertainment, **not** Productivity — the prior project used Productivity and the figures differ).
   Report the category actually used and why. Note that the population includes funded teams with
   audiences, so it flatters a solo cold-start developer.
2. **The funnel.** At candidate price points, how many paying subscribers reach $1k and $3k MRR
   **net of the store cut and net of COGS** from the unit-economics ticket? Then, at category-median
   install→paid conversion, how many installs does that require? Show the arithmetic. If a fraction
   has no credible source, **decline it and say so** rather than inventing it.
3. **The acquisition check.** LTV per install vs published CPI for the category. The prior project's
   decisive finding was that paid acquisition lost money at every price point ($0.62 vs $1.33) —
   redo it for this category. If paid acquisition is ruled out, organic is the only route and the
   developer has no audience.
4. **The treadmill.** Churn in music/audio subscription apps, and the installs-per-month needed
   **in perpetuity** to hold $1k and $3k. Steady-state matters more than the peak.
5. **The addressable-audience sanity check.** Combine the surviving platform's user base (note the
   Tidal ticket's concern — permissive but small may be commercially pointless) with the fraction who
   are podcast listeners, commuters, and willing to pay a third audio subscription. Do not
   manufacture the fractions; where sourceless, say so.
6. **Price pick**, anchored to the real competitor. If the closest competitor is free or a platform
   feature, anchor to free, not to the priciest indie app.
7. **The steelman**, stated at full strength: the strongest honest case that the bar **is** reachable.
   Then say why it does or does not hold.

Conclude: is $1k likely, is $3k plausible, and what would have to be true.
